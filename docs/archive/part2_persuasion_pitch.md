# Part 2 Persuasion Pitch

## Stakeholder Hat

UN Food Systems Summit Panel or a regional food-security policy board.

## Core Message

Food price shocks do not hurt everyone equally. Australia and New Zealand producer prices are used as a supply-side case study, while the exposure layer identifies countries structurally less able to absorb global food-price pressure.

## 5-Minute Pitch Arc

### 1. Hook: The Human Problem

"A food price rise can be a budget inconvenience in one country and a crisis trigger in another. The question is not only whether prices rise, but who has the least capacity to absorb the shock."

Visual: `Overview`

Purpose: Establish the human-centered story and dashboard scope.

### 2. What Is Happening: Producer Price Signals

Show AUS/NZ producer price trends and the FFPI overlay.

Visual: `Price Trends`

Talking points:

- AUS/NZ producer prices move over long time periods and respond unevenly by commodity.
- Crisis bands highlight 2007-2008, 2010-2011, and 2022.
- This is the supply-side signal, not the whole global food system.

### 3. So What: Global Food Price Context

Show FFPI sub-indices, crisis-year markers, and GHI context.

Visual: `Global Context`

Talking points:

- FFPI shows the global shock landscape.
- GHI shows why equal price pressure creates unequal human consequences.
- Correlation with FFPI sub-indices helps identify which commodity groups align more strongly with global price shocks.

### 4. Who Is Most Exposed: Hunger-Import Exposure Matrix

Show the corrected exposure matrix.

Visual: `Exposure Matrix`

Talking points:

- This is not a bilateral AUS/NZ trade-flow model.
- It is a structural exposure index based on hunger severity and food import dependency.
- We replaced multiplication with a normalized weighted index to avoid exaggerating the relationship.

### 5. What Next: What-If Scenario

Use the producer shock and pass-through sliders.

Talking points:

- A producer-price shock does not become an import-price shock one-for-one.
- Pass-through is adjustable because shipping, exchange rates, policy, margins, and substitution affect final import costs.
- The tool helps prioritise monitoring and preparedness, not make causal forecasts.

## Recommended Call to Action

"Use hunger-import exposure as an early-warning layer. When producer prices and FFPI move together, food-security teams should prioritise monitoring countries that combine high hunger severity with high food import dependency."

## Q&A Defence

**Why not direct AUS/NZ trade exposure?**

We do not currently have bilateral trade-flow data. The dashboard avoids claiming direct dependence and instead identifies structural exposure to global food-price pressure.

**Why use a weighted index instead of multiplication?**

Multiplication assumes equal compounding and exaggerates countries where both variables are high. We now min-max scale both inputs and weight GHI more heavily because hunger severity is the direct human-centered risk.

**Why include a pass-through slider?**

Producer prices are not import prices. Transport, exchange rates, trade margins, policy buffers, and substitution mean only part of a producer-price shock may reach importers.

**Is this a forecast?**

No. It is an illustrative scenario and prioritisation tool.
