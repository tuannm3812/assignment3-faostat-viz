Four steps that turn the charts into an early-warning routine.
Every month

Check oils and cereals

Compare both with a year earlier when the FAO index is released early each month.

Input: two numbers from the FAO release
Owner: Analyst, 15 minutes
When the signal fires

Move the 20 countries forward

Start with the hungriest importers: Madagascar, Haiti, Niger.

Trigger: oils and cereals both above +20%
Owner: Programme leads
Every year

Refresh the watch list

Add new hunger and import data; Haiti, Kenya and Benin are trending worse.

Trigger: each new Global Hunger Index
Owner: Policy board
Before relying on it

Back-test on unseen years

Fix the rule on data to 2012 and score it on 2013 onwards.

Output: an honest hit rate on unseen shocks
Owner: Data team

Three limits to keep in mind, and where to explore the data

What the evidence can and cannot support, and the interactive version of this story.
1

The rule was read off its own history

The +20% line and the two prices were chosen by looking at 2000–2026, so the 4 of 5 score is in-sample. A test on unseen years comes first.

2

Snapshots, not live data

Hunger is one 2025 score and import shares are the latest year from 2015–2024. Exposure can change faster than the data.

3

World prices are not shop prices

Local currency, subsidies and stocks change how much of a world spike reaches a household. Local price data would sharpen the list.

Data and definitions

Sources, thresholds and how each measure is built.
FAO Food Price IndexMonthly price index of a basket of food commodities, 2014–16 = 100. Five groups: cereals, vegetable oils, dairy, meat, sugar. Jan 1990–Mar 2026.
Shock monthAll-food index more than 20% above the same month a year earlier. Runs with gaps of up to three months are joined; runs longer than six months are major shocks.
Two-price signalVegetable oils and cereals both more than 20% above a year earlier, in a month that is not already a shock month. A hit is a shock starting within 12 months.
Global Hunger IndexCombines undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting and child mortality; 20–34.9 is serious. 98 countries scored in 2025.
Food import shareWorld Bank: food imports as % of merchandise imports, latest year from 2015 per country. Median across the 91 countries: 16.3%.
Exposed countryGHI 2025 of 20 or more and a food import share above the median: 20 countries.
Farm-gate pricesFAOSTAT producer price index for Australia and New Zealand, median across 118 products per year; flagged outliers removed.
Built withPython and pandas for the data, matplotlib for the charts, Streamlit and Plotly for the dashboard, Looker Studio for the shared view, and the UTS deck kit for layout (Google Sans, Viridis for data).
