Three limits to keep in mind, and where to explore the data

What the evidence can and cannot support, and the interactive version of this story.
1

The rule was read off its own history

The +20% line and the two prices were chosen by looking at 2000–2026, so the 4 of 5 score is in-sample. A test on unseen years comes first.

2

Snapshots, not live data

Hunger is one 2025 score and import shares are the latest year from 2015–2024. Exposure can change faster than the data.

3

World prices are not shop prices

Local currency, subsidies and stocks change how much of a world spike reaches a household. Local price data would sharpen the list.

IN SHORTThe signal is a prompt to prepare, not a forecast. Test it on unseen years before money depends on it.

Definitions

The thresholds behind every measure in the main story.
Shock monthAll-food index more than 20% above the same month a year earlier. Runs with gaps of up to three months are joined; runs longer than six months are major shocks.
Two-price signalVegetable oils and cereals both more than 20% above a year earlier, in a month that is not already a shock month. A hit is a shock starting within 12 months.
Exposed countryGHI 2025 of 20 or more and a food import share above the 16.3% median: 20 of 91 countries.
Global Hunger IndexCombines undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting and child mortality. 20–34.9 is serious; 35–49.9 is alarming.
Food import shareWorld Bank: food imports as % of merchandise imports, latest year from 2015 per country.
Farm-gate pricesFAOSTAT producer price index for Australia and New Zealand, median across 118 products per year; flagged outliers removed.
